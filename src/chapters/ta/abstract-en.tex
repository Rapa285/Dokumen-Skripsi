	\small
	Electronic Health Records (EHR) are a fundamental component of healthcare delivery in Indonesia. Ministry of Health Regulation No.\ 24/2022 requires all healthcare facilities to implement EHR systems that guarantee the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of patient data. To address this requirement, prior research developed DecMed, a decentralized EHR management framework built on IOTA Distributed Ledger Technology, Proxy Re-Encryption (PRE), and IPFS. While DecMed successfully resolved access-control concerns, its existing activity logging mechanism falls short of a proper audit log: logs are scattered across components without a central collector, event coverage is narrow, old records can be overwritten, formats are unstandardized, and digital evidence is vulnerable to erasure through DLT data pruning.

	This thesis develops the \textbf{Audit Log Service (ALS)}, an independent backend service integrated into DecMed's architecture. The design begins with STRIDE threat modeling of DecMed's Data Flow Diagram, producing 25 threats that are mapped to 13 audit event types (EV1--EV13). Each event source (Patient Client, Hospital Client, Ministry Client, and PRE Server) delivers audit events to ALS asynchronously as Ed25519-signed payloads. ALS verifies the digital signature, forms a structured audit record, and applies SHA-256 hash-chaining between records to ensure tamper-evidence on the local log file. Periodically, ALS rotates the active log file, uploads the archive to IPFS Cluster with pinning to prevent Garbage Collection removal, and publishes rotation metadata as a frozen object on a Move smart contract on the IOTA network via the Gas Station Server already available in DecMed.

	Implementation results show that ALS successfully aggregates audit events from all DecMed components into a single centralized repository, captures all 13 event types covering every identified STRIDE threat, detects log tampering through cryptographic hash-chaining, and ensures long-term data retention through a combination of IPFS pinning and immutable on-chain metadata. ALS does not alter DecMed's existing business logic and reuses the available IPFS and IOTA infrastructure.

	\textbf{\textit{Keywords: audit log, DecMed, IOTA, IPFS, hash-chaining, digital signature, STRIDE, electronic health record}}
